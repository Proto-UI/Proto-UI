The explicit expected value is never omitted or derived from a tracking ref, and cannot be empty. The only admitted successful transaction advances that exact existing tip to its direct child. ## Dispatch fence and exact-commit semantics

Staging a publication intent is the controller's durable reservation point: its existing exact-tip CAS both validates the claimed generation and fences it. This uses the existing intent commit, preserving the four-commit normal publication budget. If a competing material admission wins first, intent persistence fails and no POST occurs. After reservation, new target-PR wake-ups remain durable in `deferred` instead of advancing the reserved generation. Other PRs can still queue. An already visible deferred material wake-up stops the one-time attempt before dispatch; byte-identical or unchanged-material wake-ups do not invent a generation. Once dispatch has begun, later target wake-ups are logically ordered after it. Receipt finalization releases the slot and applies them in journal order, coalescing pending work without dropping it. Unknown outcomes retain the fence and deferred work, with no takeover, expiry or retry.

This serializes cooperative journal admissions with dispatch; it does **not** lock GitHub's PR head, discussion, permissions or policy. Final live collection and canonical checks run under the reservation. A GitHub update can still occur after the final read. The [review API](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/pulls/reviews#create-a-review-for-a-pull-request) binds `commit_id` to the reviewed commit, not a compare-and-swap on the current head. Both canonical rendered evidence and the helper result identify that reviewed commit only. `AGENTS.md:120` requires a fresh boundary read; `contributor-agents.md` requires a later head to be reviewed incrementally; `authorizeReviewMerge` requires the eligible publication receipt to match the live head. Existing governance therefore does not credit an old-head review as approval of a newer head. No new policy exception or perfectly atomic external-head prerequisite is introduced.

After a confirmed review receipt, the helper immediately reads the current PR head. `reviewedHeadSha` and `observedHeadSha` are distinct result fields. A changed head is queued as a read-observed `synchronize` hint only while the separate event scope remains active, using a `post-review:<receipt-id>:<observed-head>` delivery ID; it is not represented as a verified webhook delivery. This costs at most one additional journal entry for that observed update. The parent should immediately drain `followUpQueued` with a fresh session, a new delivery ID (do not reuse the already recorded post-review ID), current collection and its own incremental judgment. It must not reuse the prior packet or interpret `status: published` as approval of the observed newer head.

If observation is unavailable or the event scope is paused, the confirmed receipt remains a publication of the reviewed commit and the result carries `followUpRequired` plus `followUpError`; no alternate scope, dismissal or retry is attempted. The parent must retain that result and handle the follow-up debt. A changed head arriving after the observation remains the responsibility of the existing event subscription; no instantaneous quiescence guarantee is made. Same-head deferred discussion updates also require follow-up. Any uncertain journal acknowledgement remains fail-closed under the existing global unknown-outcome rule.

GitHub-native stale-approval dismissal/counting is separate from the canonical controller's exact-head merge gate. Current branch-protection settings could not be inspected through this connector (403), so no automatic stale-approval dismissal is claimed. Changing `dismiss_stale_reviews`, last-push approval rules or other branch protection, or dismissing a review, would require separate authority; none is changed or needed for this controller repair. Independent review and deployment readiness remain separate from the executed local race tests.

## Proven pre-attempt cancellation and covered sweep members

Final live-input, permission, policy, assessment or already-deferred-material rejection can occur after the intent is durably acknowledged but before its one-time publication attempt is consumed. That original process may append `cancelPublicationIntent`, bound to the exact unchanged intent and its own still-owned slot. The local/remote adapter rejects cancellation after attempt consumption, after an uncertain journal write, while another candidate awaits confirmation, or from a restarted/non-owning process. This cleanup does not require adopting a newly changed policy and cannot submit a review. Once attempt consumption succeeds, even a later pre-POST policy rejection remains conservatively `unknown`; a thrown submit, missing receipt, or uncertain attempt acknowledgement never supplies cancellation evidence.

A confirmed cancellation returns `status: cancelled`, releases the global slot, drains deferred commands in journal order, and preserves pending work and the prior analysis/publication baseline. It does not report the rejected packet as a completed analysis or a publication. The staged intent and cancellation remain immutable journal entries; the session is consumed and cannot retry. The parent may drain pending work with a fresh session, fresh collection and current authorization. Only an attributable no-write CAS conflict permits a bounded re-read/retry of cancellation, at most three attempts against the identical intent; uncertain cancellation acknowledgement or exhausted contention returns `unknown` and is never retried. The cancellation replaces the receipt entry in the four-advance lifecycle budget. No cancellation command is exposed to parent frames, and no takeover or missing-review inference is added.

Each completed analysis retains the material digest, admitted generation and starting delivery ID of its claimed material, all derived from verified journal replay. If an initial-sweep enqueue finds that exact material already durably analyzed, it atomically records both the fixed sweep delivery and completion of that captured PR. It preserves the original event-scope analysis rather than relabeling its provenance. Equal material still in flight, merely enqueued, abandoned, cancelled or held behind an unknown result cannot count as terminal analysis. Other pending generations and PRs remain queued.

New connector sessions add `sweepCoverageVersion: 1` to `finishAnalysis` and `stagePublicationIntent`; callers do not select it in parent command frames. Those are the only journal commands accepting the optional field, and every other value rejects. Publication finalization inherits the version of its persisted intent, never the version of a later reader. This extension changes neither the canonical packet nor its rendered review body/digest.

For a version-one terminal analysis, a captured member is complete when the analyzed material belongs to the same material run observed by its fixed initial-sweep delivery, or to a legitimately admitted later run of that PR. The reducer records the starting delivery only when material is admitted and binds its generation to the claim during canonical analysis validation. It locates the sweep's required run from that PR's verified delivery order, including deferred deliveries that have not yet become admitted material. Therefore an old fenced A cannot cover a later deferred C, and A → C → A cannot mistake the old A analysis for the later A generation merely because their digests match. A later B that actually reaches `finishAnalysis` or confirmed receipt finalization can complete the captured member after A was superseded; it does not become an A analysis/receipt. No inflight, abandoned, cancelled or unknown-intent state supplies that proof.

The original fixed sweep delivery, its digest, inventory and event provenance remain immutable. Reusing its ID with changed evidence still rejects; a completed member skips before recollection and makes no second intake or claim. Field-less historical finish/stage commands retain their original exact-material and initial-source completion rules, including later commands that were valid under those rules. This is replay compatibility, not migration: an already-stalled old member or old ABA outcome is not automatically repaired or reinterpreted. A newer reader can replay old commands; an older reader rejects the new field rather than ignoring it. All active workers/readers must use the accepted pinned implementation before new version-one commands are written. No old journal, receipt, owner or deployment is rewritten or adopted.

## Confirmed receipt persistence under contention

A confirmed connector receipt is not made into an ambiguous review POST merely because a later journal enqueue wins before finalization. The original process may attempt receipt persistence at most three times, each time re-reading the latest revision and verifying the entire same fenced intent before applying the identical receipt. Only an attributable no-write `conflict` result is retryable. This includes the initial revision check and one exact local Git porcelain stale-lease rejection bound to the attempted candidate, ref and expected tip. The latter attempted a Git push but proved no remote state update was applied. The original owner must read the current revision, verify the identical fenced intent and construct a fresh exact-parent candidate, never replay the rejected commit. Other remote rejections, malformed or unbound/multiple porcelain rows, signals, network or lost-acknowledgement outcomes remain `unknown` and stop mutation. Changed intent or lost owner also stops. The review POST is outside the loop and remains one-shot.

Successful reconciliation releases the slot and preserves all intervening target and other-PR work. If uncertainty or the three-attempt contention budget stops reconciliation after the receipt was confirmed, the result retains `publicationConfirmed: true`, the exact receipt and reviewed head while overall persistence remains `unknown`. This is evidence for attended reconciliation, not permission for another process to adopt the owner, repeat the POST, dismiss a review or reset the ledger. Definitive pre-write conflicts add no journal entry, so the normal four-advance publication budget remains unchanged.

## Intent-free rejection and distinct review baselines

Before staging starts, collection, identity/permission, assessment, policy and deterministic ledger-binding failures cannot have dispatched a review. The consumed session re-reads the journal and may release only its original, unchanged, intent-free claim through `abandon`, retaining pending work. A definitive no-write staging CAS conflict uses the same cleanup. The helper still throws the original rejection; failed cleanup adds an explicit `claim release unknown` reason. Only a proven no-write cleanup conflict permits another fresh read and candidate, at most three attempts. An ambiguous staging result, thrown staging call or failed acknowledged-intent readback never supplies this proof, even if a later read appears intent-free. The adapter's process-owner and stopped-mutation checks remain mandatory. This is neither takeover nor retry of the rejected publication.

The same terminal rule applies to analysis-only `finish`: collection, identity, policy, read and deterministic packet/generation-binding rejections release only the unchanged original intent-free claim. A definitive no-write finish CAS conflict also releases that claim instead of retrying the analysis. The original rejection still reaches the worker as an `error`; only an acknowledged `finishAnalysis` returns `status: applied`. An applied `abandon` is a claim release, never evidence that the rejected packet became a durable analysis. Pending work, deferred work and both prior analysis/publication baselines remain intact. An unknown finish acknowledgement or thrown write is not proof of non-completion and triggers no automatic release or retry.

Explicit `abandon` uses that same original-owner helper, returning its acknowledged release revision and retrying only proven no-write conflicts, at most three attempts per command. Consuming a session for finish/publication is distinct from consuming the one-time publication attempt: if a proven pre-write rejection's cleanup failed only because its read was unavailable or its conflict budget ran out, the retained original session may later receive an explicit `abandon` to release the identical intent-free claim. A changed owner/claim, persisted intent or adapter-stopped uncertain write still blocks it. This does not allow a new finish/publication attempt, restarted-owner adoption or an automatic retry of an ambiguous write.

Material coverage includes `baseSha` and `baseRefName` as well as the head and existing discussion/content fields. A changed base collected during an eligible event or initial sweep creates new material; an old event analysis cannot complete that changed sweep member. This does not admit a base-update event or expand either authorization. Historical digests remain unchanged in the journal; their first new collection may conservatively require fresh analysis. `reopened` is a distinct supported lifecycle hint in both intake and replay, never relabeled `opened`. No webhook registration, listener or policy activation is introduced.

The parent request exposes two independently replayed artifacts: `priorAnalysis` is the latest completed analysis, including `finish`-only work; `priorPublishedAnalysis` is the latest analysis with a confirmed publication receipt, or `null`. Only confirmed finalization updates the latter. Finishing an unpublished analysis must not overwrite it. Canonical v2 packets, published bodies and their digests are unchanged.

For `finish`, the packet continues to reconcile `priorAnalysis.packet` sequentially. For `publish`, its canonical `packet.reconciliation` reconciles `priorPublishedAnalysis.packet` when present, otherwise `priorAnalysis.packet`. If those two durable packets differ, the publish command must also supply `analysisReconciliation`, with the same five-field structure as canonical reconciliation, binding the latest completed analysis's exact digest/head and classifying all its prior findings. It is checked against the same current packet's actual findings: unresolved findings must remain present, resolved findings must be absent, and all current/new findings must be classified. Omitting it, substituting a digest string, forging either pointer, or hiding a finding rejects before staging. When the baselines coincide or there has been no publication, omit this extra field or use `null`; a redundant separate reconciliation is rejected.

```json
{
  "kind": "publish",
  "packet": {},
  "assessment": {},
  "analysisReconciliation": {
    "priorReviewedHeadSha": "<latest-analysis-head>",
    "priorPacketDigest": "<latest-analysis-packet-digest>",
    "resolvedFindingIds": [],
    "openFindingIds": ["B1"],
    "newFindingIds": ["C1"]
  },
  "output": "/tmp/publication-result.json"
}
```

The placeholders are not a valid packet or authorization. The worker forwards this optional envelope field, and each new session writes it explicitly (including `null`) into the staged journal command. The reducer derives `publishedAnalyses` from confirmed historical finalizations; it does not rewrite a genesis, command, receipt or packet. Field-less historical commands retain their original sequential validation for immutable replay. The current publication session always uses the explicit dual-baseline path and the unchanged canonical live-history gate. Thus published A → finish-only B → restart → publish C requires C to account for both A and B independently; multiple unpublished analyses remain a sequential chain, and the first publication after such a chain reconciles its latest analysis.

Local tests cover these sequences, cross-scope/head/base changes, malformed or missing reconciliation, journal-prefix preservation, pre-staging rejection and unknown-result boundaries, and actual stale-lease races against disposable bare Git remotes. These remain fixture/local-Git evidence, not production review, journal, webhook or deployment evidence.

## Own review delivery before receipt finalization

A review webhook can arrive after the POST creates its review but before the publisher records its authenticated receipt. Ignoring it from the unconfirmed intent would lose evidence; admitting its raw digest and draining it unchanged would instead leave a self-created pending generation. The version-one material projection resolves the ordering at confirmed finalization, without deleting or rewriting the original delivery.

New connector sessions add `reviewMaterial` only to `enqueue`. Its exact shape is `{ version: 1, input, reviewIdentities }`: `input` is a fully validated canonical v5 snapshot for the journal repository/PR; the sidecar contains one exact `{ nodeId, id, authorId }` row per review in input order. Numeric review/actor IDs are strings or explicit `null` when unavailable, node IDs must match the canonical review, and known numeric review IDs must be unique. Extra/missing fields, unsupported versions, target mismatches and raw material digest mismatches reject. The existing scheduling fields remain head/base/ref, state/draft/body, commits/files and complete reviews/comments/replies/threads; review rows additionally include their identity sidecar in the raw digest. Checks and collection timestamps remain outside scheduling material, while canonical publication validation retains the unfiltered full input.

Before confirmation, a changed projected delivery is durably deferred behind the same dispatch fence. Finalization still requires the original process owner, exact intent, matching response/readback ID, actor, target, reviewed head, disposition and body. New sessions persist `receiptNormalizationVersion: 1` only on `stagePublicationIntent`; the validated analysis/intent retains that exact version. No parent command selects it, and missing fields keep the historical finalization rules. Version one additionally requires a nonempty response node ID equal to the exact readback node ID and retains that full confirmed receipt in the replay-derived receipt record. A later collection can omit an own review from scheduling material only if repository, PR, numeric ID, node ID, actor ID/login, commit, disposition and exact body all match one such durable receipt. A review ID hint, matching account/body alone, unknown actor, intent, transport result awaiting persistence, missing receipt, simulation or unconfirmed outcome cannot supply that proof.

The reducer recalculates only versioned material during admission and deferred draining, subtracting only each fully matched own review. A pure self echo therefore adds no generation after receipt finalization. Concurrent head/base/ref/body or human discussion changes remain pending; A → C → A remains a later generation even if its final digest equals the earlier analysis. Sweep coverage uses the same effective material with its existing delivery-order bound, so an older fenced A cannot complete a sweep captured after C. `followUpQueued` comes from the exact finalization candidate after this drain, rather than from the unfiltered deferred list. No additional journal transition or POST retry is introduced.

A fresh own-review wakeup skips without a journal write only after full collection proves that the effective material is unchanged and no target work is pending/deferred. Otherwise it enters normal intake and can claim pending real work. Replaying an already recorded delivery must retain its kind, target, raw digest and versioned/field-less form; new live material under that same ID rejects instead of replacing history. A distinct eligible event can admit the new material. The canonical parent review input still contains every own review.

Compatibility is explicit: field-less enqueue commands retain opaque digest admission and deferred-drain semantics; field-less stage commands retain their historical receipt-finalization rules even when read by newer code. New marked stages can follow an existing legacy pending generation without changing its lineage. Old receipt records do not gain the stronger binding or become normalizable merely because a new reader loads them. Historical commands and journal bytes remain unchanged, including an old self-echo generation already left pending. This is not an automatic migration or repair of old state. The new reader replays field-less histories, while an old reader rejects either additional enqueue or stage field. Unsupported receipt-normalization versions reject, and no other command accepts that marker. All active readers/writers must use the accepted implementation before new projected commands are written. `sweepCoverageVersion: 1` remains confined to finish/stage commands and keeps its field-less historical compatibility.

The connector tests exercise both event/receipt orders, fresh replay, concurrent real material, own-plus-human ABA, captured-sweep ordering, exact receipt mismatch controls, unknown dispatch/finalization and immutable legacy history. The material tests cover exact schema, target/digest binding, replay collisions and each receipt comparison field. The production remote ledger adapter is exercised only against isolated local bare Git repositories; connector POST/readback are fixtures. These tests make no production review, webhook, journal-write or deployment claim.

## Canonical duplicate completion and lifecycle output

An exact governed duplicate is a no-POST terminal outcome, not a failed publication to enqueue repeatedly. The session validates the normal fresh scope, principal, packet, snapshot and canonical facts, then issues finishAnalysis with duplicateReviewCompletionVersion: 1. The reducer reuses the canonical duplicate predicate against the live input and a durable original analysis/publication packet. This marked command completes only the claimed generation and applicable sweep coverage while preserving both original analysis and published baselines, including any unpublished findings. It never creates a publication receipt or attributes the existing review to this invocation. Unmarked historical journal commands retain their previous semantics; invalid versions or changed review/identity/input reject.

A draft PR may be captured and analyzed but cannot stage a publication intent for any disposition. Lifecycle admission preserves converted_to_draft as its own event kind, alongside reopened and ready_for_review. A first successful capture-initial-sweep returns the inventory verified from the persisted ledger together with its acknowledgement; the parent need not call capture twice. Conflicts, unknown acknowledgements or inconsistent readback are not confirmed inventories.
