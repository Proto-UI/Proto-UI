## #848 follow-up: independent review of available-space projection retirement

The Search layout candidate releases the preceding solve's owned available-size CSS variables before measuring the next intrinsic floating box. The original host-unit counterexample is valid: after short content fits below the anchor, longer content stays incorrectly capped below instead of selecting the larger top side. The candidate retains authored values during measurement, uses a new owned projection per solve, and retires the projection on replacement, invalidation and disposal. This does not establish native zoom or hit testing.

Independent review added `packages/modules/positioning/test/available-projection-ownership.test.ts`. Three controls passed immediately: authored value/priority restoration, a newer author override surviving disposal/late middleware, and an equal successor's projection surviving the previous owner's retirement. A real custom element's synchronous `style` attribute callback then exposed two successive defects:

1. Disposal while the prior size projection was being restored caused the suspended `position()` call to reacquire fixed/top/left preparation after retirement. The source now rechecks the computation's current generation immediately after projection and strategy-pin retirement.
2. Disposal during `setProperty('--proto-ui-anchor-width', '50px')` left that variable behind, because the style claim was populated after the observable setter returned. The source now normalizes the planned value using a detached ordinary element's `CSSStyleDeclaration` and publishes that claim before touching the floating custom element. After the setter it checks both claim identities and the actual value/priority, so a nested dispose, replacement or author override cannot be adopted by the old continuation.

The normalizer is local to the style lease, created lazily, and does not attach an element or listener. Clearing the tested property before normalization prevents an invalid new value from borrowing a previous valid serialization. Existing inherited-before/priority handling and identity-based restoration are preserved. This review does not claim zero allocation or a performance gain from the normalizer.
